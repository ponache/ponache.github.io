B2C · Yandex Eats

Скидки и Рост

A collection of discount and growth mechanics for Yandex Eats — from a coupon system to distributing value across the entire user journey. I designed the flow for coupons and coupon sets, transparent discount communication in the Cart and Checkout, and tools that raise the average order value without cutting conversion. Separately, I designed the visual for the Russian market and its adaptation for international ones, plus graphics for promo campaigns and major launches

Research and stakeholders

Every mechanic in this case grew out of research. I personally ran more than 50 in-depth interviews, over 5 studies with UX labs and more than 50 quantitative surveys with a combined sample of over 5,000 people. Hypotheses went into a metric tree and were prioritised with RICE, scenarios were unpacked with JTBD. I defended the work in front of 16 stakeholders

First look at the savings

The scenario opens with a fullscreen that explains how coupons make orders cheaper. It is the first trigger of the return loop and the entry point into the whole mechanic. The animation shows the value faster than any copy would

Entry point on the home screen

Coupons are visible from the very first screen. The banner shows the total available savings and leads into the coupon home. Entry points from the tab bar, profile, menu and checkout work in parallel, so people never lose track of their coupons between sessions. I designed two different key visuals, one for Russia and one for international markets, because the mechanic is the same while the context and currencies are not

Savings visible in the menu

Inside a restaurant menu the coupon lives in the header. People see the discount percentage, the cart threshold and how many uses are left. Dish discounts are highlighted with green prices, so the value reads before anyone reaches the cart

Coupon home

I brought every coupon, promo code and online mechanic into one place. The list shows the essentials, the detail sheet opens up conditions, minimum cart, validity and eligible venues. The core mechanic is coupon sets. They deliver value not on a single order but across a series of future ones, which lifts frequency and LTV. After launch this delivered +1% orders among both new and returning users

One tap

At checkout a coupon applies without leaving the screen. The informer shows how much this order saves and which coupon did it. If another one is worth more, a hint offers the swap

Light checkout

Buying and upgrading coupons should not require a full checkout. I built a simplified version where payment takes a single step. People stay inside the coupon scenario instead of dropping out halfway through the funnel

Coupon upgrades

Coupons can be improved. Someone arrives through an external channel, a blogger for example, gets a booster and raises the value of the whole set at once. A limited validity window creates a FOMO effect that pushes new users hard. Similar mechanics work well for Asian competitors, so I took them apart and adapted them to our flow and to the launch of new countries. On the new markets it delivered +10% orders from new users

Discounts in the cart

I rebuilt how value is distributed in the cart. Green prices and badges on dishes, deep discounts tied to payment methods, subscriptions and internal service mechanics. People see concrete savings on each item instead of an abstract total. The contribution to margin came to +1% GM

A contextual helper

I added placements that suggest how to save on this particular order. Top up to free delivery, reach a discount threshold, add an item that unlocks a promo. The helper does the math and offers the next step instead of showing the missing amount. Average order value went up

The footer

The footer appears in the menu and lives almost all the way to checkout. It used to simply state the delivery fee. I turned it into a tool with thresholds and animations that shows how much is left until cheaper delivery and which discounts already apply

Flexible logic

The footer rebuilds itself around the conditions. It works with flat pricing and with dynamic pricing alike. It covers surcharges, heavy orders, minimum cart requirements and peak demand periods, when people need to be gently redirected to other venues. The logic adapts to the vertical, retail and ready-to-eat play by different rules. The tool brought around +1% orders and GMV

Desktop

The tool was built for desktop too. Here the footer works inside the sticky cart and holds the same threshold logic at a different interface density. Every feature in this case shipped in both versions

Payment method carousel

Choosing a payment method used to live on a separate screen that few people reached. I unfolded it into a carousel right on checkout and added a promo block that shows the value of each method. The same place holds entry points into adding a new card and into local methods, from mobile money to crypto and QR payments. The distribution brought around +0.5% orders and GMV

Transparent breakdown

The cross-check shows what the total is made of. Every discount is named, points and coupons apply with a toggle, the final sum recalculates in place. Transparency works for trust here, people understand what they pay for and abandon the last step less often. Conversion into choosing a payment method went up

Graphics

A separate part of the work was graphics inside the product. I designed 3D icons for seasonal occasions, campaigns and launches of new verticals. More than 50 variants across different sizes in one consistent style, with part of the pipeline built on generative models. An emotional layer on top of all the mechanics that also guides people into the right sections better
